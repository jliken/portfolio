After launching [Shredder](/projects/sdr) at Launch 4 Change, I decided that I wanted to make my own supersonic composite rocket. Over the last few months I have been conceptualising Project Aquila: a sub-minimum diameter, composite rocket that will aim to break the UKRA single-motor H-class record of 2432m (Ben Jarvis, 2004). The rocket is simulated to reach an apogee of 3058m with a maximum velocity of Mach 1.45 - supposing I make this thing perfectly. In reality, manufacturing defects are very hard to avoid, but I hope to break 3km nevertheless.

![OpenRocket outline of Aquila.](/projects/engineering/aqu-openrocket.png)
![3D CAD model of Aquila.](/projects/engineering/aqu-aquila-cad.png)

The first flight test I want to conduct for this project is a re-flight of Daedalus II, which I used for my [L1 certification](/projects/l1-cert). The payload will be modified to include a BlueJay 2+ (and potentially a SRAD flight computer from StrathSEDS), so that I can practice integration and launch procedure with this flight computer. If the stars align, the re-design leaves room for a dual-deploy dual-separation recovery system, which I plan to use on Aquila to minimise drift radius. I have never built my own electronic recovery system before, so this is an exciting (and terrifying) test for me. I also hope to launch DAE-II from a launch tower that I'm constructing for this project. This launch tower will eliminate the need for launch lugs on my rockets, which cause asymmetric surface drag and reduce apogee. Testing the launch tower with DAE-II will give me more confidence that I can use it with Aquila.

![Modified DAE-II payload.](/projects/engineering/aqu-daeIIa-payload.png)
![DAE-II on the launch tower.](/projects/engineering/aqu-daeIIa-rail.png)

While I prepare for this flight test, I will be constructing a suite of fibreglass body tubes in-house. This prototyping will give me an idea of wall thickness, impact resistance, strength under loading, surface finish, and optimal manufacture procedure. Performing these tests through iteration is an opportunity I didn't have when constructing Shredder, so I look forward to the results.

Having tested the flight computer, my launch procedure, the launch tower, and composite manufacture procedure, I hope to construct Aquila. This rocket will be flight-tested on a less powerful motor to characterise actual performance and recovery dynamics. And then, I hope to launch the record attempt at International Rocketry Week 2027, at SARA.
