I co-lead the airframe design & development for our main competition rocket this year &emdash; Shredder &emdash; which flew at the Launch for Change (L4C) 2026 competition hosted by ARIA & Durham University, ultimately winning the competition. Shredder flew to an apogee of 4.26km at a maximum speed of Mach 1.16 (398m/s, or 1,430 km/hr), with the airframe weighing 2.9kg. Our environmental payload was a spectropolarimeter, which analysed aerosol concentrations at different heights in an atmospheric column, as well as measuring standard atmospheric variables and VOC concentration.

Shredder's airframe was designed to be as modular as possible, including a fully removable motor mount and boat-tail, avionics housing that could be completely disassembled, recovery bulkheads that could be removed from the rocket, and a nosecone housing a removable payload. This level of modularity allowed for parallel workflow and ease of maintenance and repair. Furthermore, every airframe component was manufactured in-house, allowing for a reconstruction of the entire airframe at a very low cost (less than £200).

An important focus for this rocket was design for manufacture, since endeavour had never made a fully in-house airframe before. As a physicist transitioning into engineering, this was a skill I needed to develop to pursue rocketry as a career. Going through the design, prototype, manufacture, test, finalise pipeline was more intensive than I could have managed, especially with a complex geometry like the nosecone. Furthermore, I alone worked on post-manufacture integration with the other teams, which is never an easy process, but fortunately my experience and preparation allowed for rapid progress and a well-tested integration procedure.

Extra tasks for this project have involved the production of technical documentation for UKRA team project support insurance, and community outreach, where I assisted a team of children with their entry into the UKROC competition. Designing the airframe of a competition rocket and leading co-ordination between other sub-teams has significantly aided my professional development. Furthermore, in-house manufacture of a fully composite airframe has been a fantastic learning experience.
